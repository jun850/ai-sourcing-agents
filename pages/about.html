We are building a clearer way to source fashion.

Atelix connects brand owners with materials, components and production partners through one organized marketplace. We want good suppliers to be discoverable beyond local networks—and good ideas to reach production with fewer blind spots.

Browse materials Explore suppliers

The problem is not a lack of capability. It is access.

Much of textile sourcing still depends on introductions, trade shows, private catalogs and long message threads. Product details arrive in different formats, supplier capability is difficult to compare, and the best production partner may remain invisible to a buyer outside its existing network.

This affects both sides. Independent brands lose time moving between spreadsheets, agents and conversations. Mills and factories with strong craft or production capability struggle to reach qualified buyers in new markets.

Why we are building Atelix

Our team has worked in marketplaces, product development, finance and fashion. We have seen how digital marketplaces can open demand, but also how poorly structured listings create more noise instead of trust. Atelix is our attempt to make cross-border fashion sourcing easier to understand, compare and move forward.

From an idea to a sourcing decision

Atelix keeps the process focused on usable information and the next practical step.

  1. 1

    Describe what you need

    Search the catalog, ask Athelia, or upload a tech pack or fabric reference.

  2. 2

    Compare viable options

    Review specifications, MOQ, pricing, supplier location and verification details.

  3. 3

    Move sourcing forward

    Shortlist products, contact suppliers, request quotes and place orders from one workspace.

Trust should be visible, not assumed.

Atelix reviews supplier identity, business information and listed production capability before applying verified status. Product and factory pages keep relevant sourcing facts close to the decision.

Verification is a starting point, not a substitute for due diligence. Buyers should confirm certification scope, samples, commercial terms and production requirements directly with each supplier.
