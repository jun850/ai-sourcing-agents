# Commercial and technical term mapping

Use this guidance to structure a buyer's own sourcing brief. It does not establish supplier acceptance, pricing, payment status, availability, certification, capacity, or any other commercial fact.

## Field mapping

| Buyer-stated term | RFQ destination | Handling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific item, fit, activity, construction descriptor | Product | Keep the most specific complete noun phrase. Prefer `performance yoga jackets` over `activewear`. |
| Total production run and split quantities | Quantity; Sizes and colors | Keep the total in Quantity. Preserve each stated colorway allocation in Sizes and colors. |
| Fibre blend, construction, performance finish, GSM, stretch, recovery, waterproofing | Material or construction; Technical requirements | Preserve only text the buyer supplied. Do not turn a desired performance property into a verified capability. |
| Neck labels, care labels, hangtags, logo placement, private/white label instructions | Branding | Retain as a buyer requirement. |
| Polybags, cartons, tissue, packing method | Packaging | Retain as a buyer requirement. |
| EXW, FCA, FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP | Incoterm | Record only when the buyer states it. It is not an accepted shipping term. |
| T/T, L/C, CAD, deposit/balance split, Net terms | Payment terms | Record only when the buyer states it. It is not an agreed payment term or payment state. |
| Currency plus target, ceiling, or unit basis | Target budget | Keep qualifiers such as `FOB $10 or less per unit`; never present it as a quote. |

## Reconciliation rules

- One prompt can update multiple RFQ fields together.
- Typed buyer text is authoritative. Reference-derived text can fill an empty field only when explicitly supported by the reference.
- Keep the eight guarded RFQ fields limited to product, quantity, material, certifications, colors, sizes, target budget, and timeline.
- Store advanced buyer requirements in the editable intake specification. Do not infer missing terms.
- Ask a focused question only for information that is genuinely missing or conflicts with buyer-provided evidence.
