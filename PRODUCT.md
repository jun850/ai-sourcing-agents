# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

ARCANA is a two-sided B2B marketplace serving two audiences roughly equally, with the buyer driving the money flow. The primary buyer is the apparel brand owner sourcing fabrics, trims, accessories, and OEM services from verified factories — working in a procurement/sourcing mindset, comparing materials on specs (composition, GSM, MOQ, lead time), price, and factory trust before committing budget. The supply side is fabric mills and OEM factories who list SKUs, run ads, upload verification/tax documents, and manage a storefront through the supplier dashboard. When a surface must choose, it optimizes for the brand-owner buyer: discovery, trust, and a seamless add-to-cart-to-payment path.

## Product Purpose

ARCANA is Tokopedia for fashion sourcing — fabrics, trims, accessories, and OEM service packages in one marketplace, targeted at brand owners as buyers. It exists to let a brand owner discover verified materials and factories, trust them through visible verification and clear specs, and buy directly: add fabrics or OEM packages straight to cart and pay, without the friction of traditional back-and-forth wholesale sourcing. Success is a completed sourcing-to-order journey — discovery → trust → cart → payment — with the supply side kept active enough (verified SKUs, complete profiles) that buyers always find trustworthy depth.

## Brand Personality

Modern, minimal, precise. The voice is calm and exact, never loud or salesy. Every screen should make a complex, high-stakes procurement decision feel organized and under control — the dominant emotion is calm control: sourcing is inherently complex, and ARCANA's job is to make it feel seamless, clear, and manageable. Warmth and craft come through material photography and typographic care, not through decoration or marketing energy.

## Anti-references

Not a cheap bulk-wholesale portal — no cluttered Alibaba/DHgate density, loud red banners, or low-trust visual noise. Not a generic SaaS dashboard — no purple/blue gradients, glassmorphism, identical stat-card grids, or the hero-metric template. Not trend-chasing consumer fashion — no hype-drop streetwear energy, aggressive marketing, or influencer aesthetics; this is a professional sourcing tool, not a consumer storefront.

## Design Principles

Trust is the interface. Verification badges, clear specs, factory provenance, and secure-payment signals are load-bearing UI, not garnish — the buyer commits budget only when trust is visible at the decision point.

Specs before persuasion. Show composition, GSM, MOQ, lead time, and price plainly and comparably. Let the buyer decide on facts; never lean on marketing copy to carry a sourcing decision.

Seamless path to purchase. Every discovery surface should shorten the road to cart and payment. Reduce back-and-forth; make add-to-cart and checkout the obvious, low-friction next step for both fabrics and OEM packages.

Calm under complexity. Sourcing spans many variables; the design absorbs that complexity so the buyer feels in control. Restraint, clear hierarchy, and generous space over density.

Serve both sides without confusing either. Buyer and seller surfaces share one visual system but never bleed into each other — a brand owner never sees seller chrome mid-purchase, and the supplier dashboard stays focused on getting listed and discovered.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AAA: ≥7:1 contrast for body text, keyboard navigability throughout, visible focus states, and a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative for every animation. Note: AAA is a strict bar — the current brass/gold accent on white and muted-gray secondary text must be audited and darkened where they fall short of 7:1 (large text and non-text UI may use the AAA large-text/component thresholds). Palette and type decisions inherit this constraint.
